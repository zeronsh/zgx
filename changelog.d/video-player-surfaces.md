section: performance
---
- **A video player's view shows its frames without copying them through the CPU** (zgx-video, zgx-gpu, macOS): `Player::view` takes each frame as an IOSurface pixel buffer its thread copies on the GPU (`GpuRenderer::readback_surface`, from a pool of at most four, the free ones given back at rest; a frame finding all four held, by images an app keeps, gets a buffer of its own, so the view never stalls), where it read each back to bytes and the window uploaded it again. The pixels are the same. A 1080p clip playing in a window: app CPU 22% to 14%, instructions 2.65 G to 1.36 G a second, CPU power 1.0 to 0.54 W, app GPU time lower, footprint 0.97 GB to 0.43 GB playing and 0.63 GB to 0.12 GB at rest. A script's player (`expose`) and other platforms read bytes as before. `Player::image` is still the picture shown: its pixels are read from the buffer on screen when first asked for (`ImageData::on_demand` and `on_demand_premultiplied`, new, make an image's pixels when first read; `PixelBuffer::to_rgba` reads a buffer's).
