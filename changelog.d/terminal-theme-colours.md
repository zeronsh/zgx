- **Terminals take their colours from the theme** (zgx, zgx-terminal, zgx-desktop): the tokens `terminal.foreground`, `terminal.background`, `terminal.cursor`, `terminal.cursor_text`, `terminal.selection_background`, `terminal.selection_foreground` and `terminal.ansi.0` to `terminal.ansi.15` colour a terminal where the theme sets them, and follow a theme switch, the system's appearance or a `theme =` scoped over the terminal live, scrollback included, without restarting it. Under Ghostty they go over the user's own Ghostty configuration; in the engine-drawn terminal (Linux, Windows) over its palette. No built-in theme sets them, and one left unset keeps the terminal's own colour, so an app that never sets them sees no change. VS Code themes set them from their `terminal.*` colours. Before, switching an app to a light theme left Ghostty's text in the user's dark colours, pale on white. In Rust: `zgx::terminal::TerminalColors` and `follow_theme_colors`, and `TerminalBackend::set_colors`.
